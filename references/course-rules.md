# Course Rules

These rules are distilled from the detail-page conversion course. Use them as the domain baseline for audits.

## 0. Basic Belief

- A good detail page is not judged by beauty; it is judged by whether it converts traffic into orders, leads, consultations, or purchases.
- High conversion is usually the result of many correct details, not one brilliant idea.
- Diagnose from the consumer's buying process: interest -> trust -> immediate action.

## 1. Selling Points

- Do not confuse product advantages with selling points. A selling point must be something consumers care about and that can move them toward purchase.
- Find selling points from two sources:
  - Competitor comparison: what others have, whether you also have it, and where you are stronger.
  - Search/platform data: what consumers search for with the category or product.
- Cover three selling-point types:
  - **Generic selling points**: baseline category promises, such as oil control for shampoo or eye protection for TVs.
  - **Unique selling points**: "only we have it" or "others have it, but we are stronger".
  - **Assurance selling points**: promises that common risks will not happen, such as no games, no extra fees, refund, warranty, genuine product, or safety.
- Quantity limits:
  - First content block/first mobile screen: no more than 4 selling points.
  - Full page: no more than 8 selling points.
- Default order: unique -> generic -> assurance.
- Within the same type, use search heat, demand strength, or business priority to choose order.

## 2. Page Structure

Use this structure as the default conversion skeleton:

1. **Remove doubt**: simplified selling points, sales proof, reviews, or limited offer.
2. **Solution + advantages**: expand generic, unique, and assurance selling points in detail.
3. **Limited-time/limited-quantity offer**: give a reason to act now, not later.
4. **Warm reminder + Q&A**: handle shipping, after-sales, return policy, warranty, common questions, and professional questions.

Q&A should include both ordinary pre-sales questions and professional questions. A useful ratio is ordinary:professional = 5:3.

## 3. First Screen

The first screen must retain users and make the page worth continuing.

Required elements:

- LOGO + product name
- Main headline
- 4 concise selling points
- Purchase/action CTA

Main headline rule:

- Use "core selling point + credential".
- Common credentials: sales/review proof, professional qualification, patents, years of focus, inspection report, certification, ranking.
- Keep the headline around 6-10 Chinese characters when possible; at most 2 lines.

First-screen selling-point mix:

- Default: 2 unique + 1 generic + 1 assurance.
- If only 1 unique selling point exists, use 1 unique + 2 generic + 1 assurance.
- Keep each selling-point phrase similar in length; ideally within 1-2 characters.

Visual rules:

- Use horizontal LOGO when possible.
- Separate LOGO and product name with a centered dot " · " when both appear together.
- Choose first-screen background from the product color, lightened; or from the product benefit's representative color.
- Ensure the LOGO remains visible on the background.

## 4. Images, Graphics, Fonts, and Color

Core rule: the image must express the intended meaning.

- Pictures, graphics, background colors, and fonts are all visual elements.
- Each visual element must help communicate the selling point.
- Fonts must match meaning: for example, "regular/formal" should not use an unstable or playful style.
- Product photos should preserve the core selling point even when showing secondary functions.
- Remove all decorative elements that do not support the selling point.
- Remove unnecessary colors. A single block should usually use no more than 3 colors.
- Use alternating background colors to separate modules. Two background colors alternating is usually enough.
- Avoid red, gray, and green as large background colors unless there is a strong category reason and visual proof.

## 5. CTA Buttons

Use the Fogg behavior model: B = M * A * P.

- **Motivation**: give users a reason to click. It can be gain-seeking, such as discount, gift, better self, better result; or pain-avoiding, such as reducing loss, pain, risk, or trouble.
- **Ability**: reduce click difficulty.
  - Mobile: put CTA near the lower area; use a large, long button.
  - PC: put CTA on the right side when using left-right layout.
- **Prompt**: make the button obviously clickable.
  - Use clear visual contrast.
  - Avoid hollow outline buttons that disappear into the page.
  - Use solid pointer icons when helpful: solid hand on mobile, solid arrow on PC.

High-conversion button copy has two parts:

- Prefix: action instruction, such as "现在下单", "点击查看", "现在咨询".
- Suffix: motivation, such as "领优惠", "获取方案", "查看附近门店", "远离疼痛".

The button should match the current module. If a module says there are many nearby stores, "立刻获取我家附近门店地址" is better than a generic "免费预约".

## 6. Copywriting

Use FB rather than FAB for performance copy.

- F = Feature, product attribute.
- B = Benefit, consumer benefit.
- Avoid lingering on A = Advantage when it does not help the consumer decide faster.
- Bad copy often stays at F or FA: "what we have" and "why we are good".
- Better copy connects F to B: "what this means for you".

For the main headline, combine:

- Core selling point + credential
- FB expression

Module copy should follow:

- Claim -> expanded description -> proof/example

Rules:

- Use concrete numbers when truthful. Specific numbers often feel more credible than rounded numbers.
- If sales count is small, change the statistical unit truthfully, such as customers served -> square meters delivered.
- Avoid excessive metaphor, jargon, and adjectives.
- Do not use comma-grouped scientific-style numbers in Chinese consumer copy when it weakens perception.
- Copy must convey confidence; avoid apologetic or hesitant phrasing.

## 7. Product Video

For shelf ecommerce main-image videos, use:

1. Interest trigger: scene + problem + risk/hidden cost.
2. Solution: product and core selling point, then supporting selling points.
3. Brand display: LOGO + slogan + credential sentence.

Five formal elements:

- Subtitles
- Key subtitles
- Visuals
- BGM
- Voiceover

Rules:

- Shelf ecommerce videos often autoplay muted, so subtitles are critical.
- Subtitles must contrast with background and be concise; do not copy filler words from voiceover.
- Key subtitles for core selling points and prices should be larger, bolder, or differently styled; about 30% larger than normal subtitles is a useful benchmark.
- Visuals should highlight the product and selling point. Avoid irrelevant props.
- Prefer real people when it increases trust, but match the model to product characteristics.
- Most product videos should feel life-like rather than over-staged.
- Speed up usage process shots; slow down effect/result shots.
- BGM should be audible but below voiceover, roughly 50%-60% of voiceover volume.
- Voiceover should have product exposure and vocal variation.
- Be cautious with embedded videos inside long detail pages because they may slow page loading. Add them only when text/images cannot express the selling point well.

## 8. Layout

- Layout is logic, not decoration.
- One module should usually say one thing and support one selling point.
- Highlight core information and weaken or delete secondary information.
- Use the F-pattern:
  - Put important information toward the top or left.
  - Use size, weight, contrast, and position to show priority.
- If a product feature is physically visible, use callout lines or labels to connect copy with the exact image area.
- If the brand has already achieved a selling point, make it visible. Do not bury proof in small text or unrelated photos.

## 9. Channel and Traffic Fit

Not every conversion problem is a page problem.

For early-stage businesses:

- Prefer channels with low ad-production difficulty, precise/vertical users, and willingness to share.
- If tradeoffs are needed, first give up shareability, then loosen vertical precision; production feasibility comes first.
- Before choosing a channel, confirm that the transaction loop can close.
- Set a stop-loss line. If spend reaches the preset amount and sales are still below about 30% of target, switch or retest the channel.

For scaling-stage businesses:

- Classify channels by traffic type, not just platform:
  - Search traffic: users already have demand and are choosing among options.
  - Feed/information-flow traffic: users did not plan to buy; the ad creates demand.
- If the goal is precision, prioritize search traffic.
- If the goal is scale, prioritize information-flow traffic.
- Search traffic requires keyword category, match mode, and keyword-page fit.
- Add negative non-commercial keywords to reduce waste.
- Information-flow traffic requires refined audience labels and strong outer ad materials.
- Avoid letting one traffic channel contribute more than about 30% of total revenue when stability matters.
