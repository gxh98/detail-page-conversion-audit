# Audit Checklist

Use this checklist to inspect a real product detail page or landing page.

## 1. Intake

Collect or infer:

- Product/service name and category
- Price tier and decision difficulty
- Target consumer and main purchase scene
- Traffic source: search, feed, KOL, social, private traffic, ecommerce shelf, ads, unknown
- Conversion goal: order, lead, consultation, appointment, app install, trial, add-to-cart
- Page artifact: screenshot, long image, URL, PDF, HTML, video, competitor page
- Current metrics if available: exposure, CTR, first-screen drop-off, add-to-cart, lead rate, payment rate, ROI

If any item is unknown, do not block the audit. Mark assumptions in the final report.

## 2. Conversion Chain Scan

Check whether the page answers these questions in order:

1. Is this the product/service I need?
2. Why is this better than alternatives?
3. Can I trust it?
4. Why should I act now?
5. What exactly should I do next?
6. What risks are removed?

If the page fails early, prioritize early-chain fixes before later visual polish.

## 3. Traffic-Page Fit

- Search traffic: does the first screen match the searched keyword or intent?
- Feed traffic: does the first screen quickly create demand and name the pain/benefit?
- KOL/social traffic: does the page continue the promise from the post/ad?
- Ecommerce shelf traffic: does the page support comparison and reassurance?
- B2B traffic: does it emphasize reliability, ROI, solution, and proof rather than only "free" or "cheap"?

Flag a channel problem when the page is coherent but the incoming intent seems mismatched.

## 4. Selling Points

- Are there too many product advantages without selection?
- Are the selling points proven by competitor comparison, search demand, customer concerns, or business data?
- Does the page cover generic, unique, and assurance selling points?
- Does the first screen include no more than 4 selling points?
- Does the full page include no more than 8 major selling points?
- Are unique selling points placed before generic and assurance points?
- Are assurance points concrete enough to remove common risks?

## 5. First Screen

- Does it show LOGO + product name?
- Is the main headline a core selling point plus credential?
- Is the headline short, clear, and readable?
- Are 4 concise selling points visible?
- Does the first-screen CTA tell users what to do?
- Does the background support the product and not bury the LOGO or text?
- Is the most important promise visible without scrolling?
- Does the first screen avoid information overload?

## 6. Page Structure

Inspect module order:

- Remove doubt
- Solution + advantages
- Limited-time/limited-quantity offer
- Warm reminder + Q&A

Flag:

- Offers appear before trust is built.
- Technical details appear before user need is clear.
- Proof is buried after users would already leave.
- Q&A or after-sales policy is missing for higher-risk purchases.

## 7. Images and Visuals

- Does each image express the intended selling point at a glance?
- Are visual elements consistent with the main product promise?
- Are there decorative props, colors, people, icons, or backgrounds that distract?
- Does each block use no more than about 3 colors unless justified?
- Are module background colors separated but not chaotic?
- Are red, gray, or green large backgrounds weakening conversion?
- Do fonts match the intended meaning?
- Are product details, scale, material, effect, or proof visible enough?

## 8. Copy

- Does the headline combine core selling point + credential?
- Is the copy FB rather than F/FA/FAB-heavy?
- Does each module follow claim -> description -> proof?
- Are benefits written in consumer language?
- Are numbers concrete and truthful?
- Are metaphors, empty adjectives, insider jargon, or vague claims weakening clarity?
- Does the copy sound confident?

## 9. Buttons and CTAs

- Does each CTA have a visible button shape?
- Does button copy include action instruction + motivation?
- Does the motivation match the module and user concern?
- Mobile: is the button large, long, and easy to tap near the lower area?
- PC: is the button easy to reach, often on the right in two-column layouts?
- Is the button visually obvious against the background?
- Are icons solid and useful if present?
- Are there enough CTAs at natural decision points?

## 10. Video

- Is video necessary, or is it slowing the page for no reason?
- For main-image videos, does it follow interest trigger -> solution -> brand display?
- Can the video work muted through subtitles?
- Are key selling points emphasized with key subtitles?
- Do visuals highlight the product instead of props?
- Is usage sped up and result slowed down when relevant?
- Does voiceover include product exposure and vocal variation?

## 11. Layout

- Does one module communicate one selling point?
- Is the core information placed top/left or otherwise made dominant?
- Does the layout follow an F-pattern where appropriate?
- Are proof and product image connected with labels or callout lines when useful?
- Are unimportant details visually weakened?
- Is text scannable on mobile?
- Are modules clearly separated?

## 12. Report Scoring

For each finding, assign:

- Severity: P0/P1/P2/P3
- Impact: high/medium/low
- Cost: low/medium/high
- Confidence: high/medium/low

Use confidence rules:

- High: directly visible problem and strongly tied to known conversion principles.
- Medium: visible issue but impact needs testing.
- Low: plausible hypothesis requiring user/channel/data confirmation.
